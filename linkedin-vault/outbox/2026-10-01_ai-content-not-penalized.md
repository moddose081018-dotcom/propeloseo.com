Google doesn't penalize AI content. Ahrefs studied 331,000 pages to prove it.

They analyzed 1 million pages across 100,000 SERPs. AI detection scores barely shifted across the entire top 10 — 27.1% at position one, 30.9% at position ten.

A separate study across 600,000 pages found a correlation of 0.011 between AI content and ranking penalties. That's not weak. That's noise.

So why do people keep saying AI content gets penalized?

Because what Google actually penalizes is scaled content produced to manipulate rankings. AI just makes it faster to produce that kind of junk. The tool isn't the problem. The intent is.

Semrush's 42,000-post analysis tells the real story: purely AI-generated content held position one 9% of the time. Human-written content held it 80%. Not because of a penalty — because most AI content is published without editing, without expertise, without anything a reader needs.

The August 2026 spam update hit 82% harder than normal. Every affected site had the same pattern: mass-produced pages with no editorial layer.

What we tell clients: use AI for research, drafting, and speed. Add your expertise, your data, your experience on the topic. That's not a workaround — that's just making content worth reading.
