Ahrefs tested 1,885 pages adding schema markup. AI citations moved +2.2%. That's statistically zero.

**Slide 1 — Title**
We Told a Client Schema Would Boost AI Citations. The Data Says Otherwise.
(Ahrefs study: 1,885 pages tracked, 4,000 controls, 6M URLs analyzed)

**Slide 2 — The Correlation**
Pages cited by AI are 3x more likely to include JSON-LD schema markup.

We saw the same correlation in our own audits. So we recommended a client add schema to 200 pages specifically for AI visibility.

**Slide 3 — The Experiment**
Ahrefs ran the actual test. 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD between Aug 2025 and Mar 2026. Matched against 4,000 control pages. Measured citation changes across Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, and ChatGPT.

**Slide 4 — The Results**
ChatGPT citations: +2.2% (statistically indistinguishable from zero)
Google AI Mode: +2.4% (also zero)
Google AI Overviews: -4.6% (slight decline)

Adding schema moved nothing. Our client's results tracked with this — 90 days in, citation rate was flat.

**Slide 5 — Why the Disconnect**
Schema lives on better-maintained, technically sophisticated sites. Those sites also publish stronger content, build more authority, earn more links.

Schema isn't driving citations. It's just correlated with the things that do.

**Slide 6 — The FAQPage Wrinkle**
A separate analysis of 127K AI answer panels found FAQPage schema on 58.3% of cited sources — 3.24x more likely to be cited.

Then Google killed FAQ rich results in May 2026. The schema that correlated most with AI citations lost its primary search function.

**Slide 7 — What We Changed**
We stopped recommending schema as an AI citation strategy. We still implement it — for crawlability, rich snippets where they exist, and technical hygiene.

But the citation wins we've seen come from content depth, topical authority, and original data. Markup on a thin page doesn't change anything.

**Slide 8 — CTA**
Are you investing in schema for AI visibility? What's actually moved the needle for citations on your sites?

## First comment (post within 5 minutes)
Full Ahrefs study here: https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/

Their methodology matters — they used a matched difference-in-differences design to isolate the causal effect of adding schema, not just observing correlation. Most "schema boosts AI citations" claims are correlation studies. This one tested causation.

Schema is still worth implementing for crawlability and technical hygiene. Just don't expect it to unlock AI citations on its own.
