Google created two different internets for parasite SEO on August 30. Almost nobody in SEO is talking about it.

The site reputation abuse policy — the one Google used to crack down on coupon pages, casino content, and sponsored articles riding high-authority domains — still applies in the US, UK, and most of the world.

In Europe, it doesn't anymore.

Google suspended manual actions for this policy across the entire European Economic Area on August 30, 2026. The trigger was a Digital Markets Act investigation from the European Commission.

Same tactic. Same Google. Different enforcement depending on where the searcher is.

Forbes Advisor, CNN Underscored, major news publishers hosting affiliate content — if they had a manual action, it no longer demotes them in EEA search results.

Google says the policy still exists. They still send Search Console notices. But the penalty teeth are gone in Europe.

Their replacement: "separating" the third-party section from the host domain so it ranks on its own merit over time. That's algorithmic, gradual, and unverifiable.

We have a client with traffic split 60/40 US and Europe. They compete against a publisher running affiliate content on a DR 90+ domain. That competitor's content is penalized in US results and untouched in European ones.

Same keyword. Same SERP. Different rules.

The uncomfortable question: if Google can enforce this policy algorithmically in Europe, why were manual actions necessary everywhere else?

Change my mind — is this anything other than a two-tier system dressed up as compliance?
