We stopped reporting "links built" to clients eight months ago. The number was hiding the real story.

Linkody tracked 865,000 backlinks in their 2026 study. Only 56% are still live and correct. Average lifespan: about 20 months.

Ahrefs went bigger. They sampled 2,062,173 sites and found that 66.5% of all backlinks created since 2013 are dead. Another 6.45% return temporary errors. The total "lost" number: 74.5%.

We were building 30-40 links per month for a B2B SaaS client. Solid placements. DR 50+ domains. Relevant anchor text.

After 18 months, we ran the decay audit. More than a third of the links we'd built in year one were gone. Page restructures, domain migrations, content removals — the usual reasons nobody talks about in link building proposals.

The metric we report now: net link velocity. Links gained minus links lost over a rolling 90-day window.

For that same client, the net number was roughly half of what the "links built" number showed. We were running to stay in place.

93.8% of link builders say quality matters more than quantity. But almost nobody tracks what happens to those quality links 12 months later.

The industry sells link building as an asset. The data says it's closer to a subscription — you stop investing, the asset erodes.

Have you run a decay audit on your own link building campaigns? What survival rate did you find?
