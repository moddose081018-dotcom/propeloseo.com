80% of the URLs in the top 3 positions changed during Google's March 2026 core update. We had clients on both sides.

I want to be honest about what happened.

The update rolled out March 27 to April 8. Twelve days. SEMrush Sensor peaked at 9.5 out of 10. SE Ranking measured nearly 80% URL churn in the top 3 spots.

For context, the December 2025 update hit 67% URL churn. This one was worse.

We had three clients gain significantly. Two lost 30%+ traffic.

The winners had one thing in common: deep topical coverage with strong internal linking structures we'd built over months. Not one piece of content ranking — entire topic clusters pulling each other up.

The losers had something in common too: thin supporting content around their money pages. The pages that ranked were isolated. When Google reshuffled, nothing anchored them.

Here's what made this update particularly hard to diagnose — a spam update hit right before it. Clients who lost traffic had to figure out whether they were hit for quality, spam signals, or both. We spent the first two weeks just separating the signals.

One thing I kept coming back to: Google's own guidance says ranking drops don't necessarily mean something is wrong with your site. That's true in theory. But when 80% of top-3 positions shuffle, "nothing is wrong" isn't very comforting.

What we're doing differently now:

We're stress-testing every client's content architecture. If a money page depends on a single ranking for traffic, we build supporting content around it before the next update — not after.

The sites that held through this update weren't lucky. They were structurally sound.

How did the March update treat your sites? Genuinely curious whether the topical depth pattern held for others.
