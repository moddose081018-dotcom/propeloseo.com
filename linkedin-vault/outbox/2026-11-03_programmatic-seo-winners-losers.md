We looked at 4 programmatic SEO programs — from 512 pages to 1.7 million. Three are winning. One lost almost everything.

Here's what separates them in 2026.

**Slide 1: Title**
4 programmatic SEO programs studied.
From 512 pages to 1.7M — winners vs. losers.

**Slide 2: The winners**
Wise: 1.7M+ pSEO pages → 54M monthly organic visits. 90% of all organic traffic comes from programmatic pages.

Zapier: 590K+ /apps/ pages drive 610K organic visits/month. Template and integration pages compound.

Stacc case study: 512 pages, 18 months → 11,840 clicks/month, $48K revenue.

**Slide 3: The losers**
G2 reportedly lost ~80% organic traffic by late 2025. Reddit now ranks for most comparison queries G2 once owned.

Thin pSEO directories: Google's March 2024 "scaled content abuse" policy hit hardest here. Pages with no unique data got deindexed.

**Slide 4: What winners have in common**
Every winning pSEO page has proprietary data the page can't exist without.

Wise has real exchange rates. Zapier has real integration specs. Stacc had real service pricing.

The losers had templated wrappers around data anyone could scrape.

**Slide 5: The 30% problem**
Stacc's own data: the bottom 30% of their 512 pages produced almost no traffic.

Even in a winning pSEO program, a third of your pages are dead weight. The question is whether the top 70% carry hard enough.

**Slide 6: The AI Overview threat**
Ahrefs 2026 data: AI Overviews cut top-result CTR by 58%.

Semrush: 93% of Google AI Mode sessions end without an external click (69M sessions studied).

pSEO pages that answer simple queries are most exposed. "What's the USD to EUR rate?" doesn't need a click anymore.

**Slide 7: What still works**
pSEO wins in 2026 when:
- The page solves a transaction, not just a question
- Data is proprietary and updated automatically
- The template adds context humans actually need
- Pages target queries AI can't fully resolve

**Slide 8: What to cut**
Kill pSEO pages that:
- Wrap publicly available data with no added context
- Answer queries AI Overviews handle completely
- Have had zero clicks in 6+ months
- Exist only because the template made them easy to create

**Slide 9: The bottom line**
pSEO isn't dead. But lazy pSEO is.

The bar is proprietary data + real utility. If your template could run on any dataset and look the same, Google has no reason to rank it.

What's your pSEO hit rate — what percentage of your programmatic pages actually drive traffic?
