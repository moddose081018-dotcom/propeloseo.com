"68% of Google searches end without a click." That stat is driving bad SEO decisions.

SparkToro published the most-cited number in SEO this year. 68.01% of US Google searches ended without a click in the first four months of 2026.

The panic take: organic search is dying.

But the methodology has a crack in it. The 2024 baseline (60.45%) used Datos clickstream data. The 2026 number uses Similarweb. Different panels, different users, different devices. SparkToro themselves call the comparison "a bit of apples and oranges."

The trend is probably real. The magnitude is unknowable.

More importantly, 68% is an average across all intent types. Break it down:

Informational: 74% zero-click.
Navigational: 68%.
Commercial investigation: 46%.
Transactional: 31%.

The queries where money changes hands? Clicks happen 69% of the time.

Mobile inflates the headline number too. Desktop zero-click is around 50%. Mobile approaches 77%. Most of that gap is "weather," "score," and "pizza near me" — queries that were never going to generate a website visit.

We stopped including aggregate zero-click in client reports six months ago. We now segment CTR by intent category in Search Console.

For our B2B clients, transactional and commercial queries still convert. The zero-click trend hasn't touched their revenue-driving keywords.

If zero-click is scaring you into shifting budget, check which queries are actually losing clicks first. The answer might be "mostly the ones that weren't making money anyway."

Change my mind — are you seeing transactional zero-click rising in your data?
