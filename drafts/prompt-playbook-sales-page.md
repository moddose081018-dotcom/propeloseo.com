# A Client's Site Lost Half Its Clicks in Three Months. The Problem Wasn't Missing Content. It Was Data Nobody Had Read.

*The 11 prompts I now run before touching a single page.*

---

I run an SEO agency. I don't usually publish my working files.

But a client came to me after a bad summer. What I found when I sat down with their data changed how I work. So here it is, anonymised.

## January was the best month the site ever had.

1,438 clicks from Google. Rankings climbing. The brand name sat at position 2.

Then Google's May 2026 core update rolled out between 21 May and 2 June.

Three days after it finished, clicks dropped. By August the site was at 622 a month. The best week of the year had 380 clicks. The last full week I measured had 111.

That's a 71% fall.

## The obvious fix would have made it worse.

The instinct after a drop is to publish more. New pages, longer pages, rewrites.

So before writing anything, I exported 16 months of their Search Console data and went through it properly.

Here's what was actually in it.

**1. The "non-brand traffic" they wanted to recover had never existed.**
764 generic queries. 98 clicks in sixteen months. A 0.18% click-through rate. Their main money keyword sat at position 66. You can't recover rankings a site never held. More content aimed at those terms would have been wasted.

**2. One of their best pages was switched off.**
A page sitting at position 9.3 with 4,201 impressions was set to `noindex`. It had been hidden as a "duplicate" of another page. That other page was never even built.

**3. robots.txt was blocking the site's own redirects.**
A comment in the file said the rules were there "to consolidate link equity". They were doing the opposite. Google can't follow a redirect on a URL it isn't allowed to fetch, so 29+ redirects had never worked.

**4. The pages defending the brand had almost no internal links.**
The page answering "is this site legit?" had 3 internal links. Meanwhile, boilerplate pages had over 250. Same domain, same authority. The one page with 79 links held #1 on the same search.

**5. Forty pages were on page one and nobody clicked.**
40 pages ranked at position 25 or better, with 800+ impressions and under 2% CTR. One sat at position 7.3 on 10,624 impressions and got 13 clicks. These pages didn't need rewriting. Most needed a better title.

**6. Two pages with the same visibility had a 70x difference in clicks.**
Both had about 6,000 impressions. Both were on page one. One got 1,058 clicks and the other got 15. The difference wasn't authority. Google's results already contradicted the losing page's premise, and no amount of rewording fixes that.

**7. Most of the "visibility loss" wasn't real.**
On 18 August, impressions fell from 636 to 44 in a day. Clicks barely moved. The impressions that vanished were bots, not people. Chasing them would have burned a month.

## None of this was a content problem.

It was a *reading* problem. Each one was sitting in data the site already had, and the obvious plan would have spent weeks fixing the wrong thing.

So I wrote down the exact checks I ran, as prompts. Each one tells the AI what to ask you for, what to look at, and what it's **not** allowed to make up.

That last part matters. Most SEO prompts let the AI invent search volumes, competitor moves and "best practices". These don't.

**Built-in rules every prompt follows:**
- Asks you the right questions first, then waits for your answers before analysing.
- Never invents search volume, difficulty, conversion rates or what competitors are doing.
- Flags anything that needs live search-result checking: *REQUIRES SERP VALIDATION*.
- Never claims a change will definitely improve rankings.
- Ends with a decision: **DO, TEST, IGNORE, SCALE or KILL.**
