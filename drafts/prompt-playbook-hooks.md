# Opening hooks: AI SEO Prompt Playbook

Ten openings to test on the same sales-page body (`prompt-playbook-sales-page.md`, from "January was the best month…" onward) and as TikTok first lines. Every fact is from the anonymised client data in the sales page's source table.

| # | Pattern borrowed | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Odd, specific mechanism (the "half teaspoon" move) | This client was about to publish dozens of new pages. The real problem was five lines in robots.txt. |
| 2 | Audience call-out + warning | If your traffic dropped after Google's May core update, don't write another word until you've checked these seven things. |
| 3 | Hidden-cause reveal | One of this site's best pages, sitting at position 9 on Google, had been switched off. Nobody had noticed. |
| 4 | Number contrast | Two pages. Same visibility. Both on page one. One got 1,058 clicks. The other got 15. |
| 5 | "It's not what you think" myth-bust | A traffic drop isn't always a content problem. On this site, almost none of it was. |
| 6 | Shock statistic | 764 keywords. 98 clicks in sixteen months. Here's why more content would never have fixed it. |
| 7 | "If you're like me" empathy | After a traffic drop, most of us open a blank doc and start rewriting. On this client's site, that would have been the worst move. |
| 8 | Missed-opportunity frame | Forty of this site's pages were already on page one of Google. Almost nobody clicked them. |
| 9 | Debunk the scary number | Part of this site's "visibility loss" wasn't people at all. 592 impressions vanished in a day, and clicks barely moved. |
| 10 | Light villain (industry advice) | The usual advice after a drop is "publish more". This client needed to read the data they already had. |

## Test plan
- TikTok: one hook per video, same body. Change only the hook (one variable per test, per the TikTok handover).
- Sales page: rotate the headline only. Judge on click-through to checkout, not views.
- Winner rule: same as the TikTok handover, at least 1.5x the baseline before scaling.
